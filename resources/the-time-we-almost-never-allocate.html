The Time We Almost Never Allocate

A Wellness and Wealth day at Proper Wellness at Santa Monica Proper, and why women who allocate capital so rarely allocate time to themselves.

By Lynn Fernando, Co-Founder & Managing Partner, REV Global

I spent a day at Proper Wellness at Santa Monica Proper for Wellness and Wealth. A spa day first, then time in the member lounge with women who allocate capital for a living.

I keep thinking about that lounge.

The spa hours mattered. So did sitting down afterward, still in that slower pace, with women who usually find each other across a table. I know how to show up for a meeting. I am less practiced at showing up for an afternoon that has no agenda and no deliverable.

These are women who spend their weeks deciding where money should go. That part of the work is familiar. Giving ourselves the same care we give everyone else comes harder, and I know that habit in myself. I'll protect a call. I'll protect a close. Those hours feel non-negotiable. A morning to recover still feels like something I have to justify.

If you work in capital, or around it, you already know this pattern. The work is serious, so it gets the calendar hold. Your own energy becomes the flexible line in the plan. I have done that for years. I can tell myself I'll rest when the week finally clears. The week rarely gets clear.

The way I mean wellness here is simple. It's time and attention. You give it to yourself, and you share it. You don't wait for a perfect opening. You keep a little time on purpose, and you let other women keep it with you.

A room like that feels different from a boardroom. Nobody's performing a point of view. You're just there, rested enough to actually hear each other. Trust gets built more quietly when the first thing you offer is time, not a pitch. I've left a lot of meetings with a next step and very little sense of the people in the room. I left this day with the opposite. That is the point of a day like this one. Not a speech. Just enough time, shared with the right people, to remember that the work is not the only thing that deserves care.

That is a good reminder that wellness is something you build and share, not just book. It's a big part of why I'm building Maliguna, a wellness membership club with stays in California and Baja. A place to rest, eat real food, and reconnect across wellness · farm · beach.

I'd like to see more women in rooms like that one, and more of us putting time into wellness, hospitality, and food. I want that to be part of how we live inside the work.

The women at Wellness and Wealth already know how to be serious about a business. What I loved was watching us be serious about one another, in the simplest way. A few hours that weren't about the deal.

I hope more of us get that serious about our own energy, and about sharing it with the women sitting next to us.
