# AI as a Commercial Diligence Edge

**For:** Founders, operators, and corporate development teams
**Reading time:** 12 minutes

---

Diligence has always been a time problem. The questions are known. The files are messy. The people who know the truth are busy running the company.

Generative AI does not change what a good commercial question is. It changes how fast a careful team can assemble the picture: revenue quality, GTM durability, concentration, contract shape, and the story the numbers refuse to support.

Used well, it is an edge for operators who want to walk into a deal conversation (or a board conversation that feels like one) with fewer surprises. Used carelessly, it is a fluent intern who invents footnotes.

This article is the bridge between the [GenAI library](/resources/executive-guide-to-genai) on this site and the commercial work in the [Commercial Audit](/resources/commercial-audit-for-growth-stage-operators) and [exit-readiness checklist](/resources/founder-exit-readiness-checklist). It is industry analysis and operating practice. It is not investment advice, not a promise about deal outcomes, and not a way to raise capital.

---

## What Commercial Diligence Is Asking

Strip the theater and you are left with a short list.

1. **Is the revenue real, recurring, and quality-checked?** Bookings vs. cash, one-time vs. repeating, pull-forward, and channel stuffing by any other name.
2. **Can the motion run without the people who invented it?** Founder gravity, key-person risk, and whether a new owner could staff the same GTM.
3. **Where does the number hide?** Concentration, custom contracts, discounting, and segments that only work in the average.
4. **What breaks after a change of control or a leadership change?** Customer consent, vendor terms, culture, and the first 180 days of integration.
5. **Does the data deserve trust?** If the CRM, the ledger, and the slide do not tie, everything else is a story.

AI helps you get to those questions faster. It does not answer them for you.

## Where GenAI Helps (and Where It Must Not)

### Strong uses

| Task | How to use it | Human gate |
|------|----------------|------------|
| Contract and clause sweep | Ask for unusual SLAs, termination, MFN, and nonstandard liability across a folder | Counsel reviews anything that would change a term |
| Customer and cohort synthesis | Summarize churn reasons, expansion notes, and support themes | Operators confirm the themes against the files |
| Call and email pattern review | Cluster objections, discount triggers, and "we promised" language | A seller or CSM validates the cluster |
| Model vs. narrative check | Compare the deck's claims to the backup exhibits | Finance owns the reconciling items |
| Integration question list | Generate a first-pass Day 1–180 list from the org and vendor map | The operator who has to live it edits the list |
| Interview prep | Brief a diligence interview from public materials plus your own notes | You still do the interview |

### Weak or dangerous uses

**Inventing missing files.** If the data room does not have it, the model should say so. "Helpful" completion is how fake citations get into memos.

**Valuation or "what this should trade at."** That is not what this site is for, and it is not what a general model is good at.

**Legal conclusions.** Models can flag language. They cannot give you an opinion.

**Customer-identifiable dumps into a public tool.** Treat diligence files as sensitive. Use an environment your counsel and security team have actually approved. See the data questions in the [AI Vendor Evaluation Checklist](/resources/vendor-evaluation-checklist).

**Replacing a Commercial Audit with a chat thread.** A transcript is not a scored system. If you need the constraint map, run the audit.

## A Practical Diligence Workflow

Keep the human structure. Put AI in the assembly step.

### 1. Freeze the question list

Write the ten commercial questions you will actually decide on. Examples: concentration, net retention, motion durability, pricing leakage, delivery load, key-person risk. If you cannot name the questions, you are not ready for a tool. You are ready for a scoping conversation.

### 2. Stage the files

Put source documents in a controlled workspace: financials, pipeline exports, contract samples, org chart, customer lists, and the current board or leadership pack. Label what is source vs. what is commentary.

Do not paste regulated or personally sensitive material into a consumer chatbot. If you do not know where the data goes, stop and read the [executive GenAI guide](/resources/executive-guide-to-genai) section on sensitive data.

### 3. Extract, then reconcile

Ask the model to extract facts into a table: customer, ARR, term, renewal, owner, custom terms. Then reconcile that table to finance. The value is the mismatch list, not the first table.

A useful prompt pattern (RCOF, from the [prompt playbook](/resources/prompt-engineering-playbook)):

- **Role:** commercial diligence analyst, extract only
- **Context:** these files, this date range, this definition of recurring
- **Output:** a table plus a "not found" list
- **Format:** columns you define in advance

If the model cannot fill a cell, it must say `NOT FOUND`. That instruction is the difference between a tool and a liability.

### 4. Pressure-test the narrative

Take the company's own story (deck, memo, verbal) and ask for contradictions against the extract. You want a list of claims that are unsupported, not a rewrite that makes the story prettier.

Then do the reverse: ask what important facts in the files never appear in the narrative. Silence is a finding.

### 5. Build the working session, not the verdict

Turn the mismatch list into a meeting agenda. Owners, dates, and the two or three issues that would change your mind. AI can draft the agenda. People have the conversation.

## Deal Readiness for the Company Being Examined

If you are the operator, not the examiner, the same workflow is how you stop being the bottleneck in your own process.

Prepare a commercial pack a stranger can use:

- One-page motion (see the [revenue infrastructure playbook](/resources/growth-stage-revenue-infrastructure))
- Pipeline and retention snapshot that ties to finance
- Top accounts, concentration, and relationship owners
- Pricing and exception log
- Known issues, written without spin

Then run an internal "red team" with AI as the junior analyst: give it only the pack, ask it to list holes, and fix the holes before a real outsider does.

This is also how you get [exit-ready](/resources/founder-exit-readiness-checklist) without waiting for a process. The pack is the product.

## Governance You Should Not Skip

Commercial files are a gift to a competitor and a problem for a regulator if they leak.

**Minimum bar**

- Approved tool, written data-handling rule, and a named owner
- No training on your inputs unless counsel has accepted that risk
- A human reviewer for anything that leaves the building
- A record of what was uploaded and when it was deleted
- The same skepticism you would bring to a first-year analyst: check the cites

If you are building a longer AI investment case inside the company, use the [ROI template](/resources/genai-roi-business-case) so the tool spend has a job. Diligence is a use case with a start and an end. Do not turn it into an unbounded subscription.
