# The Commercial Audit: What Growth-Stage Operators Should Check First

**For:** Founders, CEOs, and operators inside growth-stage companies
**Reading time:** 12 minutes

---

Most growth-stage companies do not stall because the market disappeared. They stall because the commercial system cannot carry the next increment of volume.

Pipeline looks busy. The founder still closes the important deals. Forecasts get rebuilt every Monday. A new hire is supposed to "take GTM off your plate." Revenue moves, then it doesn't, and nobody can point to the constraint with a straight face.

A Commercial Audit is a structured look at that system. It tells you what is actually producing revenue, what is theater, and what has to change before more spend, more headcount, or a bigger target will work.

This is the offer I run with operators who want a clear picture before they scale. No jargon tour. No 80-page deck that nobody uses.

---

## What a Commercial Audit Is (and Is Not)

A Commercial Audit is a time-boxed review of how revenue is created, converted, retained, and reported. It covers the motion, the math, the handoffs, and the operating rhythm that sits behind the number.

It is **not** a brand workshop, a fundraising pitch, or a promise that a new tool will fix a messy funnel.

It is also not a post-mortem after a missed quarter. The useful time to run one is when you still have options: before a big hire, before a new segment, before you raise the plan, or before you tell a board the engine is "ready."

| A Commercial Audit does | A Commercial Audit does not |
|-------------------------|----------------------------|
| Map how revenue actually happens today | Redesign your brand or website from scratch |
| Score readiness across pipeline, pricing, GTM, retention, ops, and data | Guarantee a revenue outcome |
| Name the two or three constraints that cap the next stage | Replace your leadership team |
| Leave you with a sequenced fix list and owners | Produce a vanity dashboard nobody trusts |
| Decide whether you are ready to scale spend | Solicit capital or speak for investors |

If you cannot describe the motion on one page, you are not ready to pour fuel on it.

## Who This Is For

This work is built for **growth-stage operators**: companies that have a real product, real customers, and a number that matters, but still run too much of the commercial motion through a few people.

Typical signals:

- Revenue is founder-led, or concentrated in one rainmaker
- The forecast is a negotiation, not an inspectable system
- Discounting is tribal knowledge
- Marketing, sales, and customer success keep different versions of "pipeline"
- New reps take too long to produce, or never produce the founder's book
- Retention and expansion are reactive
- You are about to hire, enter a new segment, or sit in a diligence process and cannot hand someone a clean commercial picture

If you are still searching for product-market fit, an audit will tell you that. It will not invent a market.

## What Gets Audited

Six areas. Every engagement uses the same spine so findings stay comparable and the fix list stays honest.

### 1. Pipeline quality and conversion

Not "how many names are in the CRM." How demand is created, how it is qualified, how it moves, and where it dies.

We look at stage definitions, conversion rates, cycle time, win/loss honesty, and whether the forecast can survive a skeptical review. A long list with no hygiene is not a pipeline. It is inventory.

### 2. Pricing and commercial terms

Price architecture, packaging, discount authority, exception paths, and contract shape. Growth-stage companies often scale volume while leaking margin through one-off deals the founder approved two years ago and never revisited.

### 3. GTM motion and ownership

Who owns the motion from first conversation to cash. Inbound, outbound, partners, and founder network are different systems. Pretending they are one "sales process" hides the constraint.

We also look at capacity: what one productive seller can actually carry, and whether the hiring plan matches that math.

### 4. Retention and expansion

The cheapest revenue you already have. Save motions, expansion triggers, QBR quality, logo vs. revenue retention, and concentration risk. A company that grows by replacing churned accounts is not scaling. It is treading water with better slides.

### 5. Commercial operations and handoffs

Handoffs between marketing, sales, delivery, and success. SLA discipline. What happens when a deal closes. Whether onboarding creates or destroys the next expansion. Most "sales problems" are operations problems that show up in the forecast.

### 6. Data and the source of truth

One number for bookings, one number for pipeline, one definition of a qualified opportunity. If finance, sales, and the founder reconcile three spreadsheets every month, you do not have a reporting problem. You have a decision-rights problem.

## What "Ready" Looks Like

Use this as a self-score before you spend more on acquisition or headcount.

| Area | Not ready | Ready enough to scale |
|------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Pipeline** | Stages mean different things to different people. Forecast is rebuilt from memory. | Named stages, conversion history, and an inspectable weekly forecast. |
| **Pricing** | Discounts by relationship. Packages exist on a slide, not in deals. | Documented price architecture, exception rules, and someone who owns them. |
| **GTM** | Founder or one seller is the motion. Playbooks live in heads. | A repeatable motion with owners, capacity math, and a ramp plan that is not wishful. |
| **Retention** | Churn is discovered late. Expansion is accidental. | A cadence for health, save, and expansion, with concentration visible. |
| **Ops** | Closed-won is a celebration, then a scramble. | Handoffs, onboarding, and coverage that do not require the founder. |
| **Data** | Three versions of the number. CRM is a graveyard. | One commercial source of truth that leadership actually uses. |

Ready does not mean perfect. It means the next dollar of spend has a place to land.

## What Usually Shows Up

I keep outcomes high-level on purpose. Pattern recognition is the point, not a highlight reel.

**The founder is still the conversion engine.** Activity looks like a team. Close rates look like one person. Scaling spend in that state buys more top-of-funnel for a bottleneck that cannot stretch.

**The forecast cannot be inspected.** Stages are labels, not exit criteria. Managers review hope. Boards hear a number that was negotiated in Slack the night before.

**Pricing leaked while volume grew.** Early logos got custom terms. Those terms became the unofficial list price. Gross margin tells the story the ARR slide hides.

**Handoffs drop revenue on the floor.** Sales promises what delivery cannot staff. Success inherits a confused customer. Expansion never starts because onboarding never finished.

**Nobody owns the commercial operating rhythm.** Weekly meetings exist. Decisions do not. The same exceptions get re-litigated. The system does not compound.

None of these require a new brand. They require a clear constraint and a sequenced fix.

## How the Engagement Runs

A Commercial Audit is designed to be usable in weeks, not quarters.

1. **Intake.** Goals, current numbers, org chart, and the question you actually need answered (scale, hire, diligence, or a stuck forecast).
2. **Artifact review.** CRM hygiene, pipeline, pricing, contracts sample, forecast, and the operating cadence as it exists, not as it is described.
3. **Operator interviews.** Short, structured conversations with the people who run the motion. Founders, sellers, success, finance. The gaps between those stories are usually the finding.
4. **Score and constraint map.** The six areas above, scored, with the two or three constraints that matter now.
5. **Readout.** A plain-language brief: what is working, what is capping you, what to fix first, and what not to spend on yet.

You leave with a sequenced list, owners, and a clear yes/no on whether the engine can carry more load. If the answer is no, you know why.

## A 20-Minute Self-Check

Work through this before you book anything. If you cannot answer half of these in writing, that is the audit.

**Pipeline**

- [ ] Can a new manager explain stage exit criteria without calling you?
- [ ] Do you know conversion and cycle time by segment, not just in total?
- [ ] Would your forecast survive someone pulling ten deals at random?

**Pricing**

- [ ] Is there a written price architecture someone other than you can defend?
- [ ] Do you know the discount distribution on the last two quarters of closed deals?
- [ ] Are exception approvals logged, or remembered?

**GTM**

- [ ] Can you describe the primary motion in one paragraph?
- [ ] Do you know what one productive seller produces by month 6?
- [ ] If you disappeared for 30 days, would the motion still produce?

**Retention**

- [ ] Do you see logo and revenue retention separately?
- [ ] Is concentration (top accounts, top segment) visible every month?
- [ ] Is there a save motion that is not "the founder calls"?

**Ops and data**

- [ ] Does closed-won have an owner after the celebration?
- [ ] Is there one commercial source of truth leadership uses in the room?
- [ ] Can you hand a buyer, a new CRO, or a board member a picture of the engine that does not require you as narrator?

If the last question is a no, you are not only unready to scale. You are unready to transfer.
